🌟 Your Rights as a Child in Zambia

The law protects you. Here are your most important rights — especially if you are ever in trouble with the police or courts.

🫂 Right #1

The Right to Be Treated with Respect

No matter what happened, you must always be treated with kindness and respect. No one is allowed to hurt you, shout at you cruelly, or humiliate you — at police stations, courts, or anywhere.

⚖️ Right #2

The Right to a Lawyer

If you are arrested or taken to court, you have the right to a free lawyer — even if your family cannot afford one. Always ask for a lawyer before answering any questions.

🛡️ Right #3

The Right to Stay Separate from Adults

If you are held at a police station or in custody, you must always be kept completely separate from adult prisoners. This is protected by law.

👨‍👩‍👧 Right #4

The Right to Tell Your Family

If you are arrested, the police must contact your parents or guardians as soon as possible. Your family has the right to know where you are and to come and see you.

🔄 Right #5

The Right to Diversion

For many first-time or minor offences, you may be diverted away from court and helped in the community instead — no criminal record.

🗣️ Right #6

The Right to Be Heard

In court and in all official processes, your voice matters. A magistrate must listen to you and consider your situation before making any decision.

🚔 What Happens if You're Arrested?

Step through each stage. Tap What if this doesn't happen? to know your rights at every point.

Step 1 of 5

At the Police Station

The police must tell you why you have been arrested. They must contact your parents or guardians right away. You must be kept in a separate room from adult prisoners.

✅ You have the right to remain silent until your lawyer arrives.

If the police don't follow these steps:

  • Tell your lawyer as soon as you see them — this is a violation of your rights.
  • Ask to speak to a Social Welfare Officer immediately.
  • If kept with adults, ask to be moved — say "I am a child and the law requires I am kept separately."
  • When released, report it to the Human Rights Commission: they investigate violations.
📞 Human Rights Commission: 0211 228265
Step 2 of 5

Social Welfare Officer Arrives

A Social Welfare Officer must be called to the police station. They will speak with you and your family, and assess whether you can go through diversion instead of court.

✅ If no Social Welfare Officer comes, ask the police to call one — this is required by law.

If no Social Welfare Officer is called:

  • Say clearly: "I am a child. The law requires a Social Welfare Officer to be present."
  • Ask a parent, guardian, or your lawyer to make the request on your behalf.
  • Do not answer substantive questions about the case until the officer arrives.
  • This is a legal requirement under the Children's Code Act.
📞 Ministry of Social Welfare: 0211 227744
Step 3 of 5

Diversion or Court?

The Social Welfare Officer and prosecutor decide together. For many first-time or minor offences, you will be diverted to a community programme instead of going to court — no criminal record.

✅ Diversion means no criminal record and a chance to make things right in the community.

If you believe diversion should apply but was refused:

  • Ask your lawyer to challenge the decision — diversion eligibility has legal criteria.
  • The Social Welfare Officer's assessment must be considered by the prosecutor.
  • You can request a review through the Legal Aid Board if you don't have a lawyer.
  • First-time, minor, and non-violent offences are eligible in most cases.
⚖️ Legal Aid Board: 0211 229631
Step 4 of 5

If You Go to Court

You will appear in a special Children's Court — a smaller, private room, less frightening than a regular court. Your lawyer will be with you. The magistrate will listen to you, your family, and all the facts before deciding.

✅ You can speak in the language you understand best — an interpreter can be provided free of charge.

If child-friendly court procedures are not followed:

  • Your lawyer can raise an objection immediately during the hearing.
  • You have the right to request an adjournment (a pause) to get proper representation.
  • If you need an interpreter and one isn't provided, ask the magistrate to order one.
  • You cannot be held with adult accused — if this happens, it must be challenged.
⚖️ Legal Aid Board: 0211 229631
Step 5 of 5

Rehabilitation & Support

Whatever the outcome, the CJF works to make sure you get the support you need — counselling, education, family support — to help you move forward. The goal is always to help you, not just to punish.

✅ The CJF goal is rehabilitation, reintegration, and making sure this never happens again.

If you are not receiving support after your case:

  • Contact the CJF Secretariat directly — this is exactly what they coordinate.
  • The Social Welfare Officer assigned to your case is obligated to follow up.
  • Your Legal Aid lawyer can advocate for access to rehabilitation services.
  • You have the right to education regardless of your case outcome.
📧 CJF Secretariat: CJF-Z@judiciaryzambia.com
